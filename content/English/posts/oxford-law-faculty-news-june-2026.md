## UK compliance with human trafficking convention

> *Hand against a metal gate.*

Research by the Bonavero Institute's Dr Marija Jovanović has been cited in the Council of Europe's latest evaluation report assessing the UK's compliance with the Convention on Action against Trafficking in Human Beings. 

Two of Dr Jovanović's papers are cited in the report. One examines how UK prisons identify and support adult modern slavery survivors and makes a series of recommendations to improve practices. The other looks at how the UK has operationalised the non-punishment principle for trafficking survivors, where unlawful acts have been committed due to exploitation or coercion. 

## Assimilated EU law in the UK 

A conference held in Oxford showcased research into how the UK is dealing with EU law 'assimilated' post-Brexit – from data protection and food safety to competition law and workers' rights. One of the key conclusions is that UK law in many areas still closely follows EU law.

In this video, Dr Johannes Ungerer explores the ongoing complexities and big picture questions about law and democracy after Brexit: who shapes the rules, how do we interpret them, and how much power should governments and courts have in rewriting this vast body of law?

## Greek detention centre conditions 

> *Sintiki detention centre, northern Greece.*  
> *Photo: Detention Landscapes*

Exclusive images and testimonies obtained by the Detention Landscapes project have revealed conditions inside the recently established Sintiki 'special detention' centre in northern Greece.

Researcher Dr Andriani Fili from Oxford's Centre for Criminology said that Sintiki's addition to the Detention Landscapes platform would help monitor human rights and document violations. 

## An exploration of personalised medicine

A new publication co-authored by the Faculty of Law's Dr Michael Morrison sets out a critical sociological approach to 'personalised medicine', highlighting its limitations and flaws but also its potential to improve medicine in the future. 

Personalised medicine (sometimes known as precision medicine) tailors healthcare to an individual patient's unique characteristics. The new book emphasises that a 'better' personalised medicine must balance technological possibility with social responsibility, equity, and an ethos of care. 

## Death penalty in Egypt

> *Close up of a person's feet tied together on sandy ground.*

541 new death penalty-related cases were recorded in Egypt during 2025, according to the latest available figures – an increase on 509 death sentences issued in 2024 and 454 in 2023. 20 executions were recorded during the same period, up from 13 in 2024 and 8 in 2023.

The report was compiled by Dr Sherif Azer, Research Associate in Oxford's Death Penalty Research Unit, as part of his role with the Egyptian Commission for Rights and Freedoms.
